Week 15 Teaching Practice

Gender Education · Alinggo S.L. Lee · Final Arc

The final week. This is not a new lesson — it's a synthesis. Alinggo must pull together 14 weeks of concepts into a coherent arc and deliver a closing that honors the journey. The Cycle of Liberation is the framework: Awareness → Reflection → Action → Transformation.

Vocabulary Quick-Fire

Synthesize (v.)
Definition: To combine various concepts into a unified whole — seeing the connections between ideas.
Try saying: "In our final presentation, we synthesized theory and practice through our board game design."
綜合 — 將各種概念結合成統一的整體。看見想法之間的連結。
Structural Literacy (n.)
Definition: The ability to see and analyze systemic patterns of power — the core skill of this entire course.
Try saying: "This course has significantly improved my structural literacy — I can now see the systems behind everyday interactions."
結構識讀 — 看見和分析權力系統性模式的能力。整門課的核心技能。
Advocacy (n.)
Definition: Public support for a cause — using knowledge, empathy, and accountability to drive structural change.
Try saying: "Effective advocacy requires a balance of empathy and accountability."
倡議 — 運用知識、同理心和問責來推動結構性改變。
Transformation (n.)
Definition: A fundamental shift in perspective that inspires new behavior and inspires others toward justice.
Try saying: "You are not leaving this course as the same person who entered. That's transformation."
轉化 — 根本性的觀點轉變，激發新行為並啟發他人追求正義。
Cycle of Liberation (n.)
Definition: The growth cycle: Awareness → Reflection → Action → Transformation. Not a line — a cycle that continues.
Try saying: "Our 15-week journey is not a line — it is a cycle. Awareness leads to reflection, reflection leads to action, and action leads to transformation."
解放循環 — 覺察→反思→行動→轉化。不是直線，是持續的循環。

W15: Final Structural Integration. This is the capstone — Alinggo delivers the semester review, the Cycle of Liberation, and the course's closing statement. This should feel like a graduation speech, not a lecture.

⏱ Suggested total time30 min
Segment 1 — The Arc

15 Weeks, One Journey

~8 minutes

Alinggo

"Welcome to our final session. Let's look back at the architecture of what we built together."

Alinggo — the four arcs

"Weeks 1–4: Concepts & Critical Thinking. We learned to see patterns — in names, stories, media, and expression.

Weeks 5–9: Identity, Diversity & Structure. We went deeper — into orientation, family, cultural norms, privilege, and tradition.

Weeks 10–13: Violence, Digital Harm & Reflection. We faced the hardest topics — harassment, body discipline, digital violence, and bystander responsibility.

Week 14: Advocacy. We turned knowledge into action through facilitation.

Week 15: Today. Integration, celebration, and responsibility."

💡 Teaching tip

Don't rush this review. Each arc should get a beat. Let students feel the weight of what they've accomplished. This is not a summary — it's a celebration of intellectual growth.

Segment 2 — The Cycle of Liberation

Awareness → Reflection → Action → Transformation

~8 minutes

Alinggo

"Our journey is not a straight line. It's a cycle of growth:

Awareness: Spotting the gendered patterns hidden in daily life.
Reflection: Understanding how those patterns shape our identity and choices.
Action: Transforming reflection into advocacy — through the board game, through your voice.
Transformation: A shift that inspires others and moves toward social justice."

覺察→反思→行動→轉化。這不是終點——是持續的循環。
Alinggo — the "aha" moment

"I want to ask you: looking back, when did you first feel your structural literacy develop? When was your 'aha' moment? How has it changed the way you see the world?"

Martin (Student)

Share a genuine reflection as a student would. This gives Alinggo practice receiving and honoring a student's growth moment.

Segment 3 — Reflection Questions

Ethics, Action & Future Commitment

~8 minutes

Alinggo — facilitated discussion

Ask these three questions, one at a time, with space for responses:

"Which of the Digital Gender Ethics Cards did you rely on most during your facilitation practice?"

"As you step out of this classroom, how will you use structural thinking in your future professional decisions?"

"What is ONE thing you want to carry forward from this course?"

Segment 4 — The Closing Statement

And That Is How Justice Lives

~6 minutes

The Final Words

This is the speech Alinggo should practice delivering with conviction, eye contact, and measured pacing. Not reading — speaking.

Alinggo

"In these 15 weeks, you have moved from basic gender concepts to awareness. From awareness to structure. And from structure to responsibility."

Alinggo

"You are not leaving this course as the same person who entered. You now carry ethical awareness, structural thinking, and advocacy skills."

Alinggo

"We do not need to change the entire world. But we can change: how we speak. How we listen. How we intervene. How we facilitate. And how we stand with others."

Alinggo

"And that is how justice lives."

💡 Delivery note

The final line is the thesis of the entire course. Say it slowly. Let the room go quiet. Hold eye contact. Then: a genuine smile. And: "Thank you." That's the last word.

Week 15 role-plays are about presentation and synthesis — the culminating performance skills.

Scenario 1
The Final Presentation Practice
Alinggo delivers a 3-minute semester synthesis.
The challenge

Alinggo picks THREE concepts from across the 15 weeks and synthesizes them into a coherent 3-minute presentation. Structure:

(1) Open: "Our course began with a simple question..." (1 sentence)
(2) Three connected concepts: Name them, link them, show how they build on each other (90 seconds)
(3) Personal growth: "What I learned is..." (30 seconds)
(4) Future commitment: "I will carry this into..." (30 seconds)

Martin gives feedback on clarity, fluency, and emotional impact.

Scenario 2
Handling a Q&A Challenge
After the presentation, a "professor" asks a tough question.
Professor (Martin)

"You mentioned structural literacy. Can you give a specific example of how this course changed the way you analyze a real-world situation?"

What to practice

Alinggo must think on her feet and give a concrete, specific example — not a generic answer. Practice being precise under pressure. The best answers connect a course concept (e.g., media amplification, gender policing) to a real-life observation.

Scenario 3
"What Will You Actually Do Differently?"
A skeptical listener challenges the practical value.
Listener (Martin)

"This all sounds nice. But what will you actually do differently tomorrow? What's the concrete action?"

What to practice

Be specific and honest: "Tomorrow I'll do something small but real. When I see a gendered joke online, I won't share it — and I'll notice why it got amplified. When a student in my class uses victim-blaming language, I'll redirect — not with shame, but with a structural question. And when I facilitate the board game in a community setting, I'll carry Do No Harm as my first principle. Structural change starts with daily choices."

Scenario 4
Delivering the Closing Statement
The final practice: deliver Alinggo's closing with conviction.
The challenge

Alinggo stands (yes, physically stands up from the chair) and delivers the full closing statement from Segment 4 — from memory, not reading. Martin rates three things:

(1) Pacing: Was each phrase given enough space?
(2) Eye contact: Did Alinggo look at the "audience"?
(3) Landing: Did the final line — "And that is how justice lives" — land with weight?

Repeat until it feels natural. This is the moment that defines the course.
