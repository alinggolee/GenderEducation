Week 9 Teaching Practice

Gender Education · Alinggo S.L. Lee

The final week brings it all together: the dynamic balance between tradition and change. Culture is not fixed — we participate in its evolution. This week requires Alinggo to hold complexity: honoring tradition while encouraging critical thinking.

Vocabulary Quick-Fire

Dynamic Balance (n.)
Definition: Finding a middle ground between old rules and new life — tradition and change existing in constant negotiation.
Try saying: "Culture operates as a dynamic balance — we can keep traditions, change them, or create new meanings."
動態平衡 — 在舊規則和新生活之間找到中間地帶。傳統與變革持續協商。
Reinterpret (v.)
Definition: To give a new, modern meaning to an old tradition — keeping the value while updating the form.
Try saying: "I want to reinterpret this norm to show that gender equality is a modern cultural value — not a rejection of tradition."
重新詮釋 — 給舊傳統賦予新的現代意義，保留價值但更新形式。
Group Consciousness (n.)
Definition: A shared sense of identity that connects individuals into a community — "we" instead of "I."
Try saying: "The Truku identity shifted from scattered clan definitions to a unified group consciousness — shaped by environmental changes and relocation."
群體意識 — 將個人連結成社群的共同認同感。從「我」到「我們」。
Agency (n.)
Definition: The capacity to act independently and make your own choices within (or against) social structures.
Try saying: "When we understand how social rules work, we gain agency — the power to choose whether to keep, change, or resist those rules."
能動性 — 在社會結構中（或對抗社會結構）獨立行動和做出選擇的能力。
Living Tradition (n. phrase)
Definition: Tradition understood not as a static relic but as something alive — continuously reinterpreted by each generation.
Try saying: "Tradition is not a museum piece. It is a living standard that we can reinterpret to fit modern values while respecting its roots."
活的傳統 — 傳統不是靜態的遺物，而是活的，被每一代人不斷重新詮釋。

Sentence Frame Drill

Frame 1 — Making a Claim:
"I believe that tradition is a dynamic balance between..."
Frame 2 — Describing Change:
"_____ caused a shift in legitimacy for many traditional..."
Frame 3 — Proposing Action:
"I want to reinterpret this norm to show that..."

💡 Final drill: Martin names a tradition (wedding customs, New Year rituals, naming conventions). Alinggo must: (1) describe the tradition, (2) identify the gender norm embedded in it, (3) propose a reinterpretation that keeps the value but updates the form. This is the culminating skill of the entire course.

W9: Tradition, Change & Gendered Identity. This is the course's capstone — everything comes together. The lesson balances respect for tradition with the power of agency.

⏱ Suggested total time30 min
Segment 1 — Opening

When Rules Fade, What Remains?

~5 minutes

Alinggo

"Last week we saw that structure is everywhere. This week we ask: what happens when structure changes? Social rules can feel like a cage. But when those rules fade, people sometimes experience a loss of standards — a feeling of 'who am I without these rules?'"

Alinggo

"Today we explore the dynamic balance between individuals and systems. We are not just managed by structure — we help it evolve."

今天我們探討個人與系統之間的動態平衡。我們不只被結構管理——我們也參與它的演化。
💡 Teaching tip

This opening acknowledges that losing tradition can feel like loss — not just liberation. That nuance prevents the lesson from feeling one-sided.

Segment 2 — Core Concept

Living Traditions & the Formation of Identity

~10 minutes

Alinggo

"Here's a key idea: tradition is not a static relic. It is a living standard. We can reinterpret it to fit modern values. This is not about destroying tradition — it's about keeping it alive by making it relevant."

Alinggo

"Look at how the Truku identity evolved. It shifted from scattered clan definitions to a unified group consciousness — not because the culture died, but because environmental changes and relocation forced a new kind of togetherness. Identity responded to context."

Martin (Student)

Ask: "Can you give me an example of reinterpreting a tradition? Like a real one, not just theory?" Push Alinggo to ground the concept in a concrete example.

Segment 3 — Discussion

The Fading Voice of the Leader

~10 minutes

Alinggo

"Traditional Truku leaders — the Bukun — once managed harmony and external relations. But modern administrative systems have shifted that authority. The elder's role has changed."

Alinggo

"Think about the elders in your own family. When their 'authority' shifts because the world has changed — does that make you feel liberated, or a little sad for the loss of tradition? Can we find our own voice while still respecting our roots?"

當長輩的「權威」因世界改變而轉移——你覺得是解放，還是有點為傳統的消逝感到悲傷？
Martin (Student) — prompts

• "I feel both — liberated AND sad. Is that OK?"
• "My grandfather's values don't match modern Taiwan at all. Should I just ignore them?"
• "How do you respect tradition without being trapped by it?"

Segment 4 — Closing

How Justice Lives

~5 minutes

Alinggo

"Culture is not fixed. It changes over time and across generations. Each of us is part of this change. When we understand how social rules work, we begin to have choices."

Alinggo

"We can choose to keep traditions, change them, or create new meanings. That is the agency we've been building toward all semester."

Alinggo

"Before we end this course — what is your opinion?"

💡 Closing tip

The final question — "what is your opinion?" — is deliberately open. Let it breathe. Don't fill the silence. In a real classroom, this is the moment students take ownership. In practice, let Martin answer as a student, and then Alinggo wraps: "Thank you. That's exactly the kind of thinking this course was designed to encourage."

Week 9 role-plays practice holding the tension between tradition and change — the hardest balance in the entire course.

Scenario 1
"Tradition Worked Fine — Why Change It?"
A student is resistant to reinterpretation.
Student (Martin)

"My grandparents' marriage worked perfectly with traditional roles. My grandmother was happy. Why do we need to change what works?"

What to practice

"And I'm glad it worked for them — truly. The point isn't that traditional arrangements are wrong. The point is that they should be a choice, not a requirement. If your grandmother chose that role freely and was fulfilled, that's wonderful. But what about someone who doesn't fit that mold? The question isn't 'should we destroy tradition?' — it's 'should tradition have room for everyone?'"

Scenario 2
"I Feel Both — Liberated AND Sad"
A student expresses genuine ambivalence.
Student (Martin)

"When I think about my family's old ways changing, I feel free but also... like something is being lost. Is that normal?"

What to practice

"Absolutely — and that feeling is exactly what 'dynamic balance' means. Change doesn't mean erasure. You can honor the values your elders taught you AND choose to express them differently. Feeling both liberated and sad isn't confusion — it's complexity. And complexity is a sign that you're thinking deeply about this."

Scenario 3
"What Do YOU Think, Teacher?"
A student turns the question back to Alinggo.
Student (Martin)

"You've been asking us what we think all semester. But what's YOUR opinion, teacher?"

What to practice

This is a beautiful capstone moment. Alinggo should share a genuine, measured response: "Fair question. I believe tradition is valuable — but it should serve the people inside it, not the other way around. I think the strongest cultures are the ones that can evolve without losing their soul. And I think every person in this room has the right to find their own balance between the roots they inherit and the life they choose to build."

Scenario 4
The Poster Talk Practice
Practice giving a 2-minute structured oral presentation.
The challenge

Alinggo pretends she's a student giving a poster talk. She picks ONE gender norm from the course (naming, expression, media, parenting, Gaya) and presents in 2 minutes using this structure:

(1) Claim: "I believe that..." (state a position)
(2) Evidence: "This is supported by..." (cite a course concept)
(3) Change: "One way to reinterpret this is..." (propose action)
(4) Reflection: "What I learned is..." (personal takeaway)

Martin gives feedback on clarity, flow, and English accuracy. Repeat with a different topic if time allows.
